The Eagle and the Fish-Packer — Denmark's Century in the Yellow Jersey

For a small country, Denmark has spent an outsized amount of time in the Tour de France's yellow jersey. Kim Andersen became the first Dane to wear it in 1983; Rolf Sørensen held it for three days in 1991 before a broken collarbone forced him out. The breakthrough belonged to Bjarne Riis, born in Herning in 1964 and nicknamed "the Eagle from Herning." In 1996, riding for Team Telekom, Riis dominated the race in the Alps and became Denmark's first Tour winner. Eleven years later, on 25 May 2007 in Copenhagen, he admitted he had done it on EPO, growth hormone and cortisone. The Tour briefly struck him from its list of champions before restoring his name with a note attached.

Riis's second act proved more durable. As manager of Team CSC and later Saxo Bank, he built the squad that carried Spain's Carlos Sastre to victory in 2008 and gave a generation of Danish riders a professional home.

The current era is cleaner and, for Denmark, far happier. Jonas Vingegaard, born in 1996 in Hillerslev in northwest Jutland, packed fish in a factory in Hanstholm while racing for a small Danish team. Signed by the Dutch outfit Jumbo-Visma, he finished second at the 2021 Tour, then won in 2022 and 2023. The decisive blow came on stage 11 in 2022, when Vingegaard attacked on the Col du Granon and Tadej Pogačar cracked, losing almost three minutes and the race in a single afternoon. Vingegaard was runner-up again in 2024, months after a serious crash at the Tour of the Basque Country.

Between Riis and Vingegaard, Denmark also lived through the Michael Rasmussen affair: the climber led the 2007 Tour in yellow until his own team withdrew him for lying about his whereabouts before doping tests.

The sport is woven into daily life. Copenhagen is built for bicycles, and in July 2022 it hosted the Tour's Grand Départ, the first ever in Scandinavia, with a time trial through the city, a stage across the Great Belt bridge, and roadsides packed far beyond organisers' expectations. Herning, Riis's hometown, had already launched the Giro d'Italia in 2012. Track racing has its own long history at Copenhagen's six-day events, and Mads Pedersen added a road world title in 2019.
