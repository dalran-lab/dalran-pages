Life in Plastic, Then "Lean On" — Denmark's Pop Exports

For a country of fewer than six million people, Denmark has spent three decades overperforming on the global singles chart. The breakthrough was Aqua, a Danish-Norwegian quartet led by the Norwegian singer Lene Nystrøm alongside Danes René Dif, Søren Rasted and Claus Norreen. Their 1997 single "Barbie Girl", from the album Aquarium, topped charts in Britain, Germany, France and much of the world and helped the record sell more than 30 million copies. The toy company Mattel sued for trademark infringement; in 2002 a United States federal appeals court dismissed the claim as protected parody and advised both sides, in the opinion's closing line, "to chill."

The next wave was subtler. Karen Marie Ørsted, who records as MØ (Danish for "maiden," and also her initials), grew up in Odsherred on Zealand and released the acclaimed debut No Mythologies to Follow in 2014. A year later she sang the hook on Major Lazer and DJ Snake's "Lean On," which became one of the most-streamed songs in history. Lukas Graham, the band fronted by Lukas Forchhammer, came from the opposite end of the spectrum: Forchhammer was raised in Christiania, the self-governing Copenhagen freetown. Their 2016 single "7 Years," a meditation on ageing and family written after his father's death, reached number one across Europe and number two in the United States.

Denmark's other pop institution is the Eurovision Song Contest, which the national broadcaster DR treats as a serious cultural event, with its own selection show, Dansk Melodi Grand Prix. Denmark has won three times: Grethe and Jørgen Ingmann with "Dansevise" in 1963, the Olsen Brothers with "Fly on the Wings of Love" in 2000, and Emmelie de Forest with "Only Teardrops" in 2013. Victory carries the duty to host. Copenhagen staged the 2001 contest at Parken Stadium and the 2014 edition in a converted shipyard hall on Refshaleøen; the latter ran tens of millions of kroner over budget and triggered a minor political scandal, with senior DR figures leaving their posts.

The through-line is a small, well-funded music-education system, the Roskilde Festival as a proving ground, and a dense network of songwriters and producers around Copenhagen who write fluently for an English-language market they were never quite part of.
