From the Filleting Line to the Yellow Jersey — Jonas Vingegaard's Tour de France

Before he wore the yellow jersey on the Champs-Élysées, Jonas Vingegaard gutted fish for a living. Born on 10 December 1996 and raised in Hillerslev, a small village in the Thy region of northern Jutland, he grew up far from cycling's traditional heartlands, in a landscape of dunes and fishing harbours rather than mountain passes. As a teenager he worked shifts at a local fish-processing factory, cycling only as a hobby alongside friends, with no clear sense that the sport could become a career. Danish cycling at the time still lived in the shadow of Bjarne Riis, the last Dane to win the Tour de France, back in 1996.

Vingegaard's rise was unusually late and unusually steep. He raced for small Danish teams before being signed by the Dutch outfit Jumbo-Visma in 2019, where his climbing talent and extraordinary engine, honed as much by instinct as by structured training, caught the attention of directors used to grooming riders from childhood academies. His breakthrough came at the 2021 Tour de France, where he finished second overall behind the Slovenian prodigy Tadej Pogačar, a result that stunned a sport accustomed to seeing Scandinavians as strong one-day riders rather than three-week contenders.

In July 2022, Vingegaard turned that promise into history. Over three brutal weeks, he out-climbed Pogačar in the Alps and the Pyrenees, most memorably on the Col du Granon, and rode into Paris as the second Dane ever to win cycling's greatest race, 26 years after Riis. He defended the title in 2023, confirming he was no one-off. His story resonated deeply in Denmark not because of nostalgia for Riis, whose victory was later clouded by a doping confession, but because Vingegaard's path, factory floor to maillot jaune, embodied a very Danish idea: quiet, unglamorous persistence rewarded on its own terms. Today, cycling clubs across Jutland report surges of young riders citing him directly, and Danish broadcaster DR treats each July's Tour as near-national event television, a legacy Vingegaard built one fish crate, and one mountain stage, at a time.
