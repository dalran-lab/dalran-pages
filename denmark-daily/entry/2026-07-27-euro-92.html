The Team on Holiday — Denmark's Euro 1992 Fairytale

It is the greatest underdog story in football history, and it is true. In the summer of 1992, Denmark won the European Championship — a team that had, quite literally, not qualified.

Denmark had finished second in their qualifying group behind Yugoslavia. But as the tournament in Sweden approached, Yugoslavia was disintegrating into war and, under United Nations sanctions, was expelled roughly ten days before kick-off. Denmark was called up to replace them. The legend — half-true, endlessly retold — is that the players were summoned back from their summer holidays, some supposedly from the beach.

Under the unflashy coach Richard Møller Nielsen, and marshalled by the giant goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel, the Danes had no expectations and nothing to lose. They scraped through the group, then met the reigning champions, the Netherlands of Gullit, Rijkaard and van Basten, in the semi-final. The match went to penalties; Schmeichel saved from Marco van Basten, and Kim Christofte slotted the winner.

The final, in Gothenburg on 26 June 1992, pitted little Denmark against Germany — freshly crowned world champions. It was no contest of spirit. John Jensen smashed in the opener and Kim Vilfort sealed a famous 2–0 victory. A country of five million had beaten the two best teams in Europe with a squad assembled almost by accident.

"Danish dynamite" entered football folklore. The story gained a poignant sequel in 2021, when Denmark reached the semi-finals of Euro 2020 after their playmaker Christian Eriksen survived a cardiac arrest on the pitch — an ordeal that once again united the nation behind its footballers. But nothing will ever match 1992: the year the team on holiday came home as champions.
