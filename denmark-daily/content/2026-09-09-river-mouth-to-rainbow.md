Aarhus sits on the east coast of the Jutland peninsula, where the short Aarhus River empties into a bay off the Kattegat. That geography is in its name: the Old Norse **Aros** meant "river mouth." Vikings founded a fortified settlement here in the 8th century, earthen ramparts went up around 934, and a bishop was seated in 948, making Aarhus one of Denmark's oldest towns. The red-brick **Aarhus Cathedral**, dedicated to Saint Clement, patron of sailors, was begun around 1190 and runs roughly 93 metres, the longest church in the country.

For centuries Aarhus was a modest market and harbour town. The turn came with industrialisation, the railway, and then **Aarhus University**, founded in 1928 as a deliberate counterweight to Copenhagen's monopoly on higher learning. Today the municipality holds about 360,000 people and the university some 38,000 students, giving the city a young average age and considerable cultural energy.

Two museums anchor its identity. **Den Gamle By** ("The Old Town"), opened in 1914 on the initiative of schoolteacher **Peter Holm**, was the world's first open-air museum devoted to urban rather than rural life. Around 75 half-timbered houses, rescued from demolition across Denmark, were dismantled and rebuilt around a cobbled market square; newer quarters now recreate a street of 1927 and one of 1974, down to a working jazz cellar. **ARoS Aarhus Kunstmuseum**, designed by the local firm Schmidt Hammer Lassen and opened in 2004, is among northern Europe's largest art museums. On its roof sits **Olafur Eliasson**'s *Your rainbow panorama*, a 150-metre circular glass walkway running through every colour of the spectrum, installed in 2011; walking it, you see the city tinted red, then yellow, then blue. It is now Aarhus's postcard image.

The city has spent two decades remaking its waterfront. The old container port became **Aarhus Ø**, a district of bold housing blocks including the jagged "Iceberg," while the wedge-shaped public library **Dokk1** opened in 2015 as Scandinavia's largest. A light-rail network, Denmark's first, began running in 2017, the year Aarhus served as European Capital of Culture. One footnote in identity politics: the city officially restored the spelling "Aarhus" in 2011, abandoning the Danish "Å" it had adopted in 1948, to make itself easier to find abroad. **Marselisborg Palace**, the royal family's summer residence, stands just south of the centre.
