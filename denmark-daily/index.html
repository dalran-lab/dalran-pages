The Holiday Team That Won a Continent — Denmark's Euro 92 Miracle

In the spring of 1992, the Danish national football team had already gone on its summer holidays. Denmark had finished second in its qualifying group for the UEFA European Championship, behind Yugoslavia, and had no reason to expect a place in the finals in Sweden. Then politics intervened. As war broke out following the breakup of Yugoslavia, the United Nations imposed sanctions, and UEFA expelled the Yugoslav team just ten days before the tournament began. Denmark, as runner-up, was handed the spot instead.

Coach Richard Møller Nielsen had to recall players from beach holidays and hastily reassemble a squad with barely any preparation. Expectations were so low that the press dubbed them "the holiday team," a nickname the players later wore with pride. Møller Nielsen's Denmark was a different animal from the flambouant, attack-minded "Danish Dynamite" side of the 1980s under Sepp Piontek, which had dazzled at the 1984 Euros and 1986 World Cup but never won a trophy. His 1992 team was more disciplined and pragmatic, built around a settled back line and the towering presence of goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel.

Denmark grouped past France and England before meeting the heavily favored Netherlands, reigning European champions led by Marco van Basten, Ruud Gullit, and Frank Rijkaard, in the semifinal. After a 2-2 draw, Schmeichel saved a crucial penalty in the shootout to send Denmark through. In the final in Gothenburg against a strong West German team, midfielder John Jensen scored a rare long-range goal, and substitute Kim Vilfort, who had been shuttling between the tournament and his seriously ill daughter's hospital bedside in Denmark, added the second to secure a 2-0 win.

The triumph remains the defining moment in Danish football history, still celebrated each June around its anniversary. It also cemented Schmeichel's reputation before his decorated career at Manchester United, and gave Denmark a model, modest preparation, tight defending, moments of individual brilliance, that the national team has returned to in later tournaments, including the emotional run to the Euro 2020 semifinal after Christian Eriksen's on-pitch cardiac arrest.
